Ask us About Bermuda

A realistic view of island life

Beautiful? Absolutely. Right for everyone? Not necessarily.

Having been raised in Bermuda and worked there for many years, I always try to give candidates a balanced view of what life on the island is really like. Bermuda is an incredible place for many people, but any international move is a major decision and the right fit matters.

“For people who embrace Bermuda for what it is, the experience can be genuinely life-changing.”

This guide is designed to give you a feel for the practicalities, the costs, the opportunities and the lifestyle before you decide whether Bermuda could be right for you.

The Island

Bermuda is just 21 square miles and shaped a little like a fish hook. Wherever you live, you are never far from the sea.

Hamilton is the island’s main business centre. Most commutes are refreshingly short: many popular residential areas are within 10–20 minutes of the city, while even longer journeys are usually measured in minutes rather than hours

Working in Bermuda

Bermuda has a long history of recruiting internationally where skills are in short supply. Overseas professionals work across healthcare, insurance and reinsurance, finance, legal, hospitality, construction, caregiving and private households.

The island has a genuinely international workforce, making it easier than many first-time expatriates expect to build a social and professional network.

Salary & Tax

Bermuda does not operate a conventional personal income tax. Instead, payroll tax applies and employers may deduct the employee portion from salary. Other deductions can include pension, social insurance and healthcare depending on the package.

We prefer to talk candidates through the expected take-home position for the specific role rather than quote a blanket percentage.

Cost of Living

Imported goods and groceries can be significantly more expensive than in the UK, although there are occasional surprises in the other direction.

The useful question is not simply “What is the salary?” but “What lifestyle will that salary give me once I am living there?” We are happy to help candidates think that through.

Bermuda feels wonderfully removed from everyday life, but it remains well connected to the UK and North America.

Travel

Direct flights connect Bermuda with London, with regular services to major US and Canadian gateways. London is roughly seven hours away; the US East Coast is around two hours by air.

Transport

Buses and ferries are widely used. Many residents commute by scooter or electric bike, and car ownership is tightly controlled compared with most countries.

Roads

The island speed limit is 35 km/h — around 22 mph. Life quite literally moves a little more slowly.

Work permits

International hires require the appropriate permission to work. The formal government processing target for standard work permits is shorter than the total relocation timeline, so candidates should still allow for recruitment checks, document collection, registration requirements and travel arrangements.

Housing

One of the biggest practical considerations.

As a rough guide, a room in a shared property may be around US$1,250–2,000 per month, while a separate one-bedroom property may be around US$2,000–3,500+ depending on standard, location and the market at the time.

Pet-friendly rentals can be harder to find, and Bermuda’s rental market is relatively small.

We have strong local relationships with estate agents and property contacts, including access through Greymane to homes that may not yet be on the open market. We also work with an experienced relocation specialist who can help with the practical side of your move where required.

That local network can make a very real difference when you are organising a move from thousands of miles away.

The Lifestyle

This is the bit people fall in love with.

Life naturally revolves around being outdoors: beaches, boating, sailing, paddleboarding, diving, golf, tennis, fishing, swimming and BBQs with friends.

The difference is that these things stop being “holiday activities”. They become part of everyday life.

You can finish work and be in turquoise water shortly afterwards. The island also has a strong community feel and newcomers often find themselves meeting people surprisingly quickly.

Be realistic

Is Bermuda right for you?

Bermuda can offer an exceptional quality of life and very good career opportunities, but it is still a small island. The cost of living is high, overseas relocation requires resilience, and you cannot simply drive to another city for the weekend.

People who need the constant scale, variety, nightlife or anonymity of a major city may eventually find island life restrictive.

“Many people arrive thinking they will stay for two or three years — and discover much later that Bermuda has quietly become home.”

The best way to approach the decision is with your eyes open. We would rather help you decide that Bermuda is not for you before you relocate than place you in a role that is unlikely to work long term.

Ask the questions that matter

Want to know what Bermuda is really like?

Where should I live? Do I need a scooter? What will groceries cost? Can my partner find work? What is the social life like? Will I feel isolated? What should I bring?

Because of our direct Bermuda experience and local connections, we can give you a realistic answer rather than simply sending you a relocation brochure.
